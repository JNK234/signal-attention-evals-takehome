# Writeup

**The finding.** Cartogram's attention agent does not fail for lack of signals. It fails at choosing which of them a human should see. Over 21 weeks it routed 294 of 629 signals to a customer success manager (CSM). Seven in ten of those met no condition the spec obliges, and it suppressed 72 signals the spec says must reach a person. One rule, a customer-visible play on an account under legal hold or an M&A quiet period, sits behind 10 of the 16 customer complaints. The two rules the agent breaks most often, an unsupported hypothesis and an ungrounded number, are not punished by any outcome. I can say this because every rule in the evaluator is the spec's own and every constant has a stated source. Every number in the three documents is printed by a script in `analysis/`.

## Methodology: how the spec became checks

**The evaluator has three layers, and nothing in it is invented.** The truth layer holds corrected telemetry, the artefact corpus, account and owner records, and the other dossiers. The check layer is 29 rules transcribed from the spec, one module per spec section. Each rule takes a dossier and the truth layer. It returns findings with the lifecycle step, the rule id, a severity, and a one-line explanation a reader can verify. The score layer turns findings into the four outputs the README asks for. The evaluator never raises, and with no context loaded it still returns the contract and marks what it could not check.

**One design rule shaped every choice: no invented rule ids and no fitted weights.** Severity classes come from the spec's own §11 table (critical, high, medium, soft), weighted 1.0, 0.6, 0.3, 0.1. A finding the evaluator cannot confirm carries half weight. Six constants are judgment rather than spec text, such as how many peer accounts make a cohort (5) and how far a cohort must move to count (20%). They sit in one block of `spec.py` labelled as such.

**Text is read by a small local model, gated by structure.** A zero-shot DeBERTa model reads each artefact for five mandatory-route triggers (cancellation, legal reference, security incident, billing dispute, champion departure) and six hypothesis topics. It runs offline from a committed score cache. A trigger counts only if the artefact exists, belongs to the account, is quoted verbatim, and was written by a customer. A quote-depth parser splits each artefact into blocks, so text under a "wrote:" line cannot fire a trigger. On 68 hand-labelled fixtures the model got 64 right, with 2 errors and 2 abstentions. The five trigger labels had 3 to 18 fixtures each. The six topic labels had 1 to 5, and that gap matters later.

**The three scores, and where their constants come from.** Quality is a product, `q = Π(1 − 0.16 × w × c)` over broken rules, where `w` is the class weight and `c` is 1 or 0.5 for certainty. The 0.16 is the slope of each annotator's own quality score regressed on the severities they recorded (−0.161, −0.162, −0.145, R² 0.60, 0.82, 0.70). Only the slope is used, because the three intercepts differ by 0.15. The product form replaced a clipped sum that put 134 dossiers (21%) at exactly zero, a floor effect by Terwee's 15% criterion. Risk is a noisy-OR over ten harm conditions, `r = 1 − Π(1 − p)`. Its tiers 0.45, 0.20 and 0.15 follow the ratio CVSS v3.1 uses between adjacent impact levels. Deserved attention is true if any of three spec conditions holds: a confirmed §8.1 trigger, a §4.6 enrichment timeout, or the agent's own P0/P1 with a non-benign hypothesis. That third condition was chosen on a pre-registered split of the annotated dossiers, 71 development and 59 held out, and tested with a permutation test (p = 0.026). The agent's own `arr_at_risk` was the strongest single predictor and I refused it, because a self-reported number must not be able to make a spec-required route look undeserved.

**An analogy for the whole design.** The evaluator is an auditor with the ledger, not a critic reading the memo. It does not ask whether the dossier sounds reasonable. It checks each claim against the books: the artefact text, the corrected telemetry, the account record. 751 tests cover the checks.

## The data: what the telemetry hid

**The telemetry file is not one row per account-day.** It holds 32,032 rows for 31,698 account-days. 334 pairs are the same day recorded twice, and the later row is always a backfill. 882 account-days are absent. 48 rows carry zero seats, zero API calls, zero dashboards and zero volume while marked `ok`, on 7 accounts. 342 rows show more active users than the contract allows. The domain guide documents three events. Legacy-collector API calls were double-counted before May 18. A latency re-instrumentation on April 27 raised p95 by about 1.8×. Rows for apac and emea are missing from June 11 to 13. Three more are not documented: the backfill duplicates, the zero rows marked ok, and a one-day shift in apac's weekly curve from bucketing on the UTC day.

**The rule I applied is refuse, never zero.** The latest `ingested_at` wins a duplicate. Legacy API calls are halved before May 18. p95 is scaled before April 27. A window that touches the ingest gap or a row that is not `ok` makes the claim unverifiable, not zero. This is the spec's own M6 procedure. The spec says why it matters: a claim that only reproduces without those steps "is an artefact of the pipeline being reported as customer behaviour."

**It changed the picture of the corpus.** 259 of 629 dossiers state a percentage change that does not reproduce on corrected data. 197 reproduce on raw data only. Six weeks hold 44% of all signals, and each is one Sunday's detector sweep hitting a whole cohort. Two are pipeline events: the May 18 migration (43 of week 21's claims are artefacts) and the June ingest gap (72 of week 24's). Four are national holidays, shown by same-weekday regional medians. On Memorial Day the median North American account fell from 35.1 active users to 7.8 while error rate and latency held. The full diagnosis and the figure are in `attention_budget.md`, section 1.

![Signals opened per ISO week, with the six burst weeks labelled by cause](figures/signals_per_week.png)

**The detectors are not high-recall, whatever the spec says.** I re-ran the spec's own thresholds on corrected telemetry. The usage_cliff detector fired on 205 dossiers. 84 match a real episode (precision 41%), and it caught 92 of 322 real episodes (coverage 29%). seat_decay: precision 51%, coverage 23%. On raw data the two detectors would see 517 and 596 episodes, so uncorrected telemetry manufactured about 195 and 140 false ones. In the text corpus, 48 written mandatory-route triggers have no dossier on the account within 14 days. Spec §3 calls the detectors "deliberately high-recall." The data disagrees.

**Two text traps mattered.** The exec_churn_language detector fires on quoted history: 125 of its 143 dossiers carry no trigger in current text. And in 17 dossiers the agent appended an email address to an otherwise verbatim quote. In 16 of them the address is not in the artefact at all.

## Annotator disagreement: three readers, one rubric

**The three annotators agree with each other only moderately, and on quality they barely agree at all.** Each labelled 240 dossiers, 130 by all three, leaving 169 unreviewed. On "deserved attention" their pairwise Cohen's kappa is 0.44, 0.43 and 0.47, which is moderate agreement after removing chance. On the 0–1 quality score, Krippendorff's alpha is 0.186 treating the scale as interval, and 0.020 treating it as nominal, a nine-fold swing from one modelling choice. Reporting raw percent agreement alone would hide all of this, so every agreement number here carries a chance-corrected statistic beside it.

**They are consistent about different things, as the README warned.** Annotator 1 is a wasted-attention reader: 62% of the flags they raised are wasted attention. Annotator 2 is a policy-and-evidence reader: 19% policy concern and 19% evidence integrity, three times the others, and the closest to the spec. Annotator 3 is generous on quality (median 0.96, never below 0.48) and flags miscalibration most. Their quality scores share one slope on severity and differ only in intercept (0.84, 0.92, 0.99), which is why the evaluator uses the slope and ignores the intercepts.

**The labels have two blind spots that no averaging can fix.** Only 6 of 606 flags are "missed signal": annotators cannot see the signals the agent never routed, and half the corpus is unrouted. And their "wasted attention" flag agrees with the outcome field of the same name only 26% of the time. Their free-text assessments come from a fixed phrase pool and sometimes contradict the dossier they sit on, so I did not mine them.

**How I handled it.** The majority vote is a check, never a training label. Before testing any candidate rule I split the 130 three-way dossiers by a hash, 71 development and 59 held out. The split earned its keep once: a candidate that scored 0.387 on development scored 0.087 held out, and was dropped. Against the majority, the evaluator's kappa is 0.12, 0.32 and 0.23 with the three annotators, below their 0.43 to 0.47 band. That gap is by construction and I keep it. The evaluator says yes only when the spec obliges. The annotators say yes to 43% of P1 signals and 6% of P2, so they largely track the agent's severity label. Adding the evaluator to the panel raises quality alpha from 0.186 to 0.295, which is the one number that says it behaves like a fourth rater rather than noise.

## Findings: what the agent does, and what predicts harm

**The agent's failure is selection, not volume.** Against the three spec conditions, its 294 routed signals score precision 31% and recall 56%. On the two quoted obligations alone (§8.1 and §4.6) that is 15% and 42%. The 5-per-week budget forced a choice in only 2 of 245 owner-weeks. All 72 deserving signals it did not route were suppressed or expired. 31 carried a written trigger, 30 had timed out waiting for enrichment, and 11 it had itself scored P0 or P1.

**One rule costs money, and the cost is an order of magnitude above the rest.** Among the 208 routed dossiers with a customer-visible play, the only place a complaint can occur, dossiers that broke §8.2 complained at 32% against 3%. Two failures can have similar frequency and costs an order of magnitude apart. §8.2 fires on 41 dossiers and produced 10 complaints. §8.6, the wrong notification channel, fires on 21 and produced 1. A complaint is the outcome the domain guide calls the clearest harm signal in the corpus, and it is the one the evaluator's risk score predicts best.

![10 of 16 complaints followed a customer-visible play on a legal-hold or M&A account](figures/complaints.png)

**No rule explains which routed signals wasted a CSM's time.** 115 of 294 were marked wasted. Among routed dossiers, every paging and materiality rule is within noise (off-hours page 44% vs 38%, below the floor 41% vs 38%). A first draft found these rules doubling waste. That was a denominator error, comparing routed against unrouted. I record it here because the same selection effect sits under every outcome field in this corpus. The waste comes from routing signals that meet no obligation at all.

**The most common findings are not harmful in outcome terms, and one of them is something else entirely.** Q2, an unsupported hypothesis, fires on 80% of dossiers and separates nothing. Churn and wasted rates match with or without it, within detector. The annotators flag a wrong hypothesis no more often on Q2 dossiers (19%) than on the rest (22%). M6, an ungrounded number, fires on 41%. The spec calls it the main way the system wastes attention. It is not: wasted rates are 40% with and 39% without. What M6 marks is exaggeration. Where the corrected data shows a real decline that the agent overstated (121 dossiers), routed accounts churned or downgraded at 53%. Where the pipeline manufactured the decline (58), they churned at 12%.

![An ungrounded number usually hides a real decline, and those accounts churn most](figures/m6_split.png)

**A policy that routes only spec obligations plus verified declines trades coverage for precision.** It routes 189 signals. Among them, later churn or downgrade is 39% against the agent's 30%. On the 108 signals both policies route, complaints are 3.7% against 5.4% and wasted escalations 27% against 39%. It reaches accounts holding 74% of realised loss against the agent's 97%, and $3.02M of loss sits at 16 accounts it never touches. The agent reached 14 of those 16 and lost them anyway. The agent's own severity ordering ranks as well as mine at equal volume (40% vs 39% churn) but drops 45% of spec-obliged signals. Whether the trade is worth it depends on what a CSM slot costs against a missed churn, which the data cannot price.

**What the scores predict.** Risk scores reach AUC 0.68 against 115 wasted escalations and 0.80 against the 16 complaints. That second number moved from 0.89 on development to 0.61 held out, and it rests on 16 events. Against churn the AUC is 0.52: the risk score does not predict churn, and I do not claim it does.

## Limitations: where this evaluation fails

1. **The least validated component supports the most frequent finding.** Q2's text test uses an uncalibrated 0.35 threshold on topic labels that had 1 to 5 fixtures each. Its benign-variation test rests on three judgment constants. A hand read of 20 sampled Q2 findings agreed with 17, disagreed with 2 and could not decide 1, so the rule is right about nine times in ten on that sample. Both misses were near-verbatim statements of the hypothesis that the scorer put at 0.23 and 0.12, below the threshold.
2. **Two scores are not independent of what they judge.** `quality_score` is computed from the violations, so it can never be evidence about a violation. `deserved_attention` reads the agent's own severity for 57 of its 163 positives and depends on which evidence the agent chose to attach.
3. **The risk score is uncalibrated and its complaint AUC rests on 16 events.** Its conditions were chosen partly on held-out performance, which is model selection even with frozen weights.
4. **The held-out split is spent.** It was read four times. Any further tuning needs fresh labels.
5. **The outcome data limits every comparison.** Complaint and wasted fields exist only for routed signals. `outcomes.jsonl` gives contradictory renewal outcomes to 92 of 176 accounts. Attribution is uncertain for most outcomes where anything happened. No policy comparison in these documents is causal.
6. **The evaluator only sees dossiers that exist.** 230 real API-call drops, 352 real seat drops, and 48 written mandatory triggers never became a dossier.
7. **The model must be downloaded for new dossiers.** The committed cache covers this corpus; elsewhere the evaluator degrades to a marked UNEVALUATED entry for the four text rules.
8. **Rules have a ceiling.** sig_0385 was a wasted escalation with zero violations and a quality score of 1.00. Some bad dossiers break no rule.

## If I had three months: a production eval for an attention layer

1. **Instrument the detection stage.** Log every detector firing, not only the dossiers that result. First-stage recall becomes a metric, and the 48 missed triggers become a dashboard number instead of a forensic finding.
2. **Make attribution a design, not a field.** Randomise routing on the margin, the signals just below the floor, and hold out a small set of accounts. Log owner actions at decision time. Without this, no policy comparison can be causal, and the trade between coverage and precision stays unpriced.
3. **Treat labels as a versioned instrument.** Rubric-anchored annotation with adjudication, ICC and PABAK reported on every batch, rubric changes handled as schema migrations with re-grading, held-out sets retired once read.
4. **Run the rules as CI.** Every spec rule is a test that runs on each agent change, with per-rule rates alerting on drift. The containment rules (§8.2, §8.3, §8.4, §8.7) gate deploys, because those are the ones with a measured cost.
5. **Calibrate the scores and state the operating point.** Isotonic calibration of risk on rolling outcomes, Brier score beside AUC, and precision reported at each owner's capacity, both pooled and per owner.
6. **Close the loop.** Each violation class gets an owner and a fix. The appended-email fabrication is one prompt change. Re-run the corpus after each fix and ship the ranking policy behind a per-owner budget, measuring complaint and wasted rates on the overlap set where both policies act.
7. **The open question.** What is one CSM slot worth against one missed churn? Until Cartogram prices that, precision and coverage can be measured but not traded. Everything above makes the measurement honest. Only that number makes the decision.
